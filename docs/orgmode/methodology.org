   <section class="bench-page-intro bench-method-intro">
   <p class="bench-eyebrow">Read the numbers</p>
   <p class="bench-lede">OptBench separates current harness scores from
   historical published averages, and keeps missing fields visible.</p>
   </section>

   <section class="bench-method-grid" aria-label="Scoring definitions">
   <article>
   <span class="bench-method-grid__index">01</span>
   <h2>PAR-2</h2>
   <p>The mean penalized cost across all runs. A successful run contributes
   the calls it used. A failed run contributes twice the problem cap.</p>
   </article>
   <article>
   <span class="bench-method-grid__index">02</span>
   <h2>Success rate</h2>
   <p>The share of recorded runs that finish successfully under the problem
   contract. Problem definitions state the convergence and cap rules.</p>
   </article>
   <article>
   <span class="bench-method-grid__index">03</span>
   <h2>Archived score</h2>
   <p>The historical archive reports the average of runs that finished under
   its cap. Failed runs are excluded, so this number is not PAR-2.</p>
   </article>
   <article>
   <span class="bench-method-grid__index">04</span>
   <h2>Ranked row</h2>
   <p>One code, one version, and one stock method on the problem's full start
   set. Short runs and adjusted methods stay off the ranked table.</p>
   </article>
   </section>

   <section class="bench-method-detail">
   <div>
   <p class="bench-eyebrow">Comparison rules</p>
   <h2>What the board ranks</h2>
   </div>
   <dl>
   <div><dt>Current harness rows</dt><dd>Ranked by PAR-2 when a complete,
   comparable per-start record is available.</dd></div>
   <div><dt>Archived rows</dt><dd>Ranked by the published primary average.
   Fields absent from the archive are shown as a dash.</dd></div>
   <div><dt>Method families</dt><dd>Rows are ranked both overall and within
   comparable families such as minimizers, minimum-mode methods, and bands.</dd></div>
   <div><dt>Band methods</dt><dd>The ranked cost is the harness force-call
   total across moving images. A per-image mean is shown when available.</dd></div>
   </dl>
   </section>

   <section class="bench-method-detail">
   <div>
   <p class="bench-eyebrow">Charts</p>
   <h2>Performance profiles</h2>
   </div>
   <div class="bench-method-copy">
   <p>A Dolan–Moré profile compares methods on the same set of starts. At a
   performance ratio of 1, the curve reports the fraction of starts on which a
   method is best. Moving right shows the fraction solved within that multiple
   of the best cost. The paper-ready family profiles use every finite ratio
   observed in the selected data; they add no smoothing or artificial failure
   threshold. A failed run remains unsolved at every finite ratio. A start
   failed by every selected method has no finite reference cost and is
   identified as omitted on the figure.</p>
   <p>Run-level figures use one lowest-ranked full row with per-start records
   for each code. Archive-only rows remain in version histories when they
   report a summary, but cannot appear in distributions or profiles without
   per-start values.</p>
   <p>History figures show the recorded PAR-2 and arithmetic mean force calls
   against the stated code version or commit, without smoothing. Distribution
   figures place every recorded start at its force-call count, use an × for a
   failed run, and do not jitter points. Success bars report the exact
   successful and total start counts.</p>
   <p>Dolan, E. D. and Moré, J. J., “Benchmarking optimization software with
   performance profiles,” <em>Mathematical Programming</em> 91, 201–213
   (2002).</p>
   </div>
   </section>

   <aside class="bench-method-note">
   <strong>About dashes</strong>
   <p>A dash is deliberate: the underlying source does not state that value.
   OptBench does not infer or fabricate missing benchmark data.</p>
   </aside>
